## Why

The Opus review of P7-32 (2026-11-04) found no blockers. The glue
module, the scope of the re-route and the determinism of the routes are
right. But two majors make routes worse than on main, and two more
block P7-35:

- **M1:** the perpendicular L ignores which side each end is on. A
  connector leaving A's right side towards a shape below and left of A
  runs back through A; other cases go through the target and spike back
  into the end. The old elbow did not. Common in flowcharts.
- **M2:** a rotated target gives a diagonal stub, so the route is no
  longer right-angled; P7-35's segment drag rejects such a segment.
- **M3:** moving the connector's body re-routes it fresh, so a Z route
  with stubs becomes a plain elbow along the shape's edge (the old
  defect), and a stored route moved together with both its targets is
  replaced, although ADR 0084 rule 1 says a move translates the path.
- **M4:** undo recomputes routes instead of restoring them: the
  command writes its re-route outside its edit, so revert cannot put
  the old rows back, and the runner then re-routes fresh. A stored
  custom route is lost on the first undo. Not a regression, but P7-35's
  "undo each step" cannot pass on this design.
- **M5:** the group test uses a parent that only translates, so it
  cannot catch a transform-order bug.

## Goal

In this order:

1. **Undo restores the rows** (M4). Each step captures the sheets its
   re-route rewrites (a snapshot edit pushed into the step, or the
   equivalent), so revert puts the old rows back exactly and redo puts
   the new ones back exactly. Drop the runner's re-route after undo and
   redo and the `moves_glue` filter: run and redo then agree by
   construction. Test: a stored custom 4-point route, move a glued
   target, undo: the rows are byte for byte the stored route; redo: the
   rows are the step's. The same for a glue and a delete on a corpus
   connector with a Visio route. Keep `undo_prop` and add seeds with a
   non-fresh stored route.
2. **A moved connector keeps its path** (M3). Connectors in the moved
   set get no re-route: the translation already wrote their path. A
   connector glued to a moved shape that is not itself moved is still
   re-routed (rule 5). Test the review's S1 (move only the connector:
   the Z route translates, stubs kept) and S1b (the connector and both
   targets moved together: the stored route translates). Redo agrees
   (item 1).
3. **The L clears its shapes** (M1). When the corner of the
   perpendicular L falls behind either stub, route a 2- or 3-corner
   detour as the wrap does. Pass the two shapes' boxes to the router so
   the wrap and the detour clear the shapes, not only the end points
   (review minor 3; or correct the wrap's doc if you keep it, and say
   why). Tests: the review's three probes (A = x 3..5, y 3..4; begin
   (5,3.5) +x to end (4,1) +y; begin (4,4) +y to end (2,6) −x; begin
   (0,0) +x to end (−3,−3) −y) give routes that never enter either box
   and have no 180° turn. Extend the routing proptest: no segment
   crosses the interior of either box, and no 180° turn.
4. **Exits are axis-aligned** (M2). Snap each exit to its dominant axis
   before building the stub. Extend the proptest to non-axis-aligned
   exits: every segment is horizontal or vertical.
5. **Groups that rotate and flip** (M5). Tests with a parent group that
   rotates 90° and one that flips: draw inside the group, glue, unglue,
   move the group, move the connector (`OneDMoveEdit`), undo and redo;
   the endpoint cells are in the parent's coordinates and the drawn
   ends sit on the targets' points.
6. **Connectors are never targets** (review minor 1). `connector.glue`
   and `connector.draw` refuse a 1-D shape as a target, the connector
   itself included, for every source, with a clear error. Test from a
   programmatic source.
7. **No self-loop of zero length** (review minor 2). When both ends
   target the same shape, they resolve to distinct candidates. Test it:
   the route has at least two distinct points and no repeated point.
8. **The drawing glue rule** (review minors 4 and 5). A press inside the
   outline of a shape with no fill takes dynamic glue, as the old
   bounds test did; a hit on a group member maps to its top-level
   ancestor, as the render doc says; a text block larger than the shape
   does not glue outside the outline. A tie between connection points
   goes to the topmost shape. Test each.
9. **Directions come from DirX and DirY only** (review minor 6). Drop
   the A/B fallback in the connection-row lookup; an extended row's A
   and B are not directions. Test an extended row.
10. **The docs** (review minors 7 and 9). The four "page inches"
    places say the parent's coordinates. Amend ADR 0084: rule 2's
    cached value is in the parent's coordinates; rule 5's "deleted"
    case keeps the stored route of a connector whose target is deleted
    (its ends do not move).
11. **The UI tests** (review minor 10), real input, both layouts:
    shapes placed so the route has stubs and bends, checked on the
    rendered picture; the picture checked in the connection-point and
    over-a-connector tests; a connector lying over a shape with the drop
    on it glues to the shape underneath.

**What changed**

1. **Undo restores the rows** (M4). The re-route pass records each
   connector's sheet before and after its write in a `RouteEdit`
   snapshot (`crates/commands/src/builtins/connector.rs`) that folds
   into the step it belongs to (`with_routes` at every call site), so
   revert puts the old rows back byte for byte and redo the new ones.
   The runner's re-route after undo and redo and the `moves_glue` filter
   go away (`crates/commands/src/builtins/view.rs`,
   `crates/commands/src/undo.rs`): run and redo agree by construction.
   Tests: a stored custom 4-point route survives a glued target's move,
   its undo and redo; a corpus connector's Visio route survives a glue
   and a delete with their undos; undo_prop seeds carry a non-fresh
   route and stay green.
2. **A moved connector keeps its path** (M3). The scoped pass skips
   connectors in the moved set (their edit translated their rows, rule
   1) and only a forced scope (glue, unglue, style) re-routes a
   connector outright. S1 and S1b: moving only the connector, and
   moving it with both targets, translates the stored route, bends and
   stubs kept, with exact undo and redo.
3. **The L clears its shapes** (M1). The join takes the first candidate
   whose full route turns no 180 degrees and enters neither glue
   target's box: the two one-corner elbows in the exit order, the
   two-corner channels, the three-corner wraps and the four-corner
   staples. The options carry the two targets' boxes (`with_boxes`) and
   the callers pass them. The review's three probes clear both boxes
   with no 180 turn; the proptest covers jittered exits and two
   separated boxes (no interior crossing, no 180 turn, every segment
   horizontal or vertical, no repeated point).
4. **Exits are axis-aligned** (M2). `unit_exit` snaps each exit to its
   dominant axis before the stub.
5. **Groups that rotate and flip** (M5). The parent chain composed
   backwards in `page_transform` (each local transform ran after its
   parent's), invisible under a translating parent. Tests with a
   90-degree parent and a flipping one: glue, unglue, move the group,
   move the connector (`OneDMoveEdit`), undo and redo keep the endpoint
   cells in the parent's coordinates and the drawn ends on the targets'
   points. A connector inside a moved group re-routes: its ends changed
   in the page even though its rows stand still (the scope's second
   category).
6. **Connectors are never targets** (minor 1). `target_shape` and
   `run_draw` refuse a 1-D shape as a target, the connector itself
   included, for every source, with a clear error; tested from a
   programmatic source.
7. **No self-loop of zero length** (minor 2). When both ends target the
   same shape and the choice is free, the resolution takes the nearest
   pair of distinct candidates (and a point-glued end excludes its own
   row): the route has at least two distinct points and no repeated
   point.
8. **The drawing glue rule** (minors 4 and 5). A press inside the
   outline of a shape with no fill takes dynamic glue (the bounds
   fallback, as the old bounds test); a hit on a group member maps to
   its top-level ancestor; a text block larger than its shape does not
   glue outside the outline (the check is the hit shape's own box); a
   tie between connection points goes to the topmost shape. Each rule
   has a tool test.
9. **Directions come from DirX and DirY only** (minor 6). The `A`/`B`
   fallback in the connection-row lookup goes; an extended row takes the
   nearest side's normal. Tested with an extended row.
10. **The docs** (minors 7 and 9). The four "page inches" places for
    connector end points say the parent's coordinates. ADR 0084: rule
    2's cached value is in the parent's coordinates, and rule 5's
    "deleted" case keeps the stored route (its ends do not move); a
    translated connector keeps its translated path and a group member
    re-routes. Amended 2026-11-04.
11. **The UI tests** (minor 10), real input, both layouts: shapes
    offset so the route has stubs and a bend, checked on the rendered
    picture (both legs inked, the hollow clear); the picture checked in
    the connection-point and over-a-connector tests; and a connector
    lying over a shape with the drop on it glues to the shape
    underneath.

**Deviations and notes**

  writes values only (the rows and the cached endpoint points), and a
  recompute would replace cached values the file kept — recalc's

  first draft hit.
- The `singular()` guard is unreachable through the transform
  construction (the scale is always plus or minus one); its test covers
  the helper's contract directly and says so.
- Item 5's tests needed the transform-order fix first: the review's M5
  suspected exactly that bug and it was real.
- The geometry-rows map into the connector's own box as before; the
  endpoint cells take the parent's coordinates (rule 2 as amended).
